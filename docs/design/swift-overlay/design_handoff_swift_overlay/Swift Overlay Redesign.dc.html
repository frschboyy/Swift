Swift · on-screen interface · redesign

Present when needed, gone when not.

One surface family that grows out of a small presence capsule, reads like Windows 11, and gives every kind of moment its own unmistakable shape: an answer is light glass, a consequence is solid with an amber band, a question wears a listening ring.

01

Design direction

Three ways to answer “calm, glanceable, trustworthy.” All three fix the type, contrast and hierarchy; they differ in where Swift lives and how much it asserts itself.

1aCapsuleRecommended

A small pill is Swift’s body. Everything grows out of it toward the screen centre and folds back into it. Translucent Windows 11 glass for quiet things, solid surfaces for things with consequences. Native-feeling, low-footprint, and it works in any corner.

1bEdge ribbon

A thin status ribbon docked to a screen edge; answers slide out as sheets. Never overlaps work, very calm. But it fights “drag anywhere”, sits far from where you’re looking, and reads as a taskbar app.

1cCompanion orb

A large expressive orb with speech-bubble cards. Very clear “Swift heard you”, warm personality. Costs space at all times, bubble tails break in mid-screen positions, and the orb competes with the content.

Why 1a
It is the only option that is small by default, honest about state in every corner, and native to Windows 11 (glass, 8–14 px radii, Segoe UI Variable).
Three shapes, three meanings
Glass card = information. Solid card with amber band = a click changes something. Blue listening ring = Swift is waiting for you. Never conveyed by colour alone: each has its own header text and icon.
Hierarchy, fixed
Your words (quiet, top) → status (one line, icon + verb) → the answer (largest type on the card) → actions (bottom, labelled). Nothing raw or internal is ever shown.

Names follow the existing styles.css where they map (--panel, --panel-edge, --raise, --text, --muted, --faint, --accent, --ok, --bad, --warn, --radius, --ease). New tokens are marked new. Theme follows Windows app mode by default; both themes keep text ≥ 4.5:1 against the panel even over white or black wallpaper, because the panel is ≥ 86% opaque with a 24 px blur.

Capsule
44 h, 120–512 w (hugs content). Orb 24. Idle dot 12.
Card
512 w; default max 340 h. Long answer open: max min(70vh, 720) – capsule; body scrolls, head and foot stay.
Chip
280 × 56. Max 2 chips + front card (3 running).
Stack
Closed 280 × 80. Open 320 w, max 6 rows, then scrolls.
Typing box
512 × 52 (+36 when a notice row shows).
Hit targets
Buttons 32 (36 on confirm), icon buttons 32 × 32, min gap 8.
Clickable vs click-through
Click-through
Everything not painted, gutters, shadows, halos, the idle dot, “Got it”, “heard you” and transcribing capsules.
Clickable
Card and chip bodies (drag from anywhere that isn’t a control), buttons, capsule while speaking (stop) or paused (resume), stack.
Never focus
All windows WS_EX_NOACTIVATE; clicks act without activating. Wheel scrolls the long answer without focus. Only the typing box activates.
Keyboard
Global: Esc stops speaking, then closes the front card. Confirm keys only via global hotkey while the card is on screen (Ctrl+Shift+Space = talk to it).

Placement
6 anchors from the tray; drag anywhere and the position is kept per monitor. After a drop, if the card’s centre is in the top half it grows down, otherwise up. Horizontal alignment follows the nearest third. Chips sit on the side of the card nearest the screen centre; the stack goes to the corner of its own drop point.
Reduced motion & contrast
prefers-reduced-motion: no translate or scale; 120 ms opacity only; heard pulse becomes a static ring; spinners become a static ¾ arc. forced-colors: Canvas / CanvasText surfaces, ButtonText borders, amber band becomes Highlight.
Theme
data-theme="dark|light" on the root, default from Windows AppsUseLightTheme. Panel opacity never below .86 so contrast holds over any wallpaper; backdrop-filter is decoration, not required for legibility.
